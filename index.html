One Job A Month, And Nothing Measuring Why

Prepared for Jeong Kim · 2 October 2026 · AG1 Consulting
What You Told Our Team
The brief, in your words
  • You currently do about one job a month.
  • You would like to get to two.
  • You are open to trying something on a trial basis.

⭐ That is a much smaller ask than most people bring us, and it is the reason this is worth a conversation. You are not asking to triple. You are asking for roughly twelve more jobs across a year — one a month. That is a different kind of problem from “double my volume”, and it is a more solvable one.

The One Number That Decides Everything

We have deliberately not guessed this. Your site lists nine different trades, and the answer is completely different depending on which ones actually pay your bills.

Gutters, attic insulation, a small repair——smallest

So the first question on the call is simply: what is a typical job worth to you? ⭐ The reason it matters is that one extra job a month is either a rounding error or a transformation, and which one it is decides whether we should be working together at all. If the honest answer is that your jobs are small repairs, we will say so rather than sell you something that cannot pay for itself.
What We Found On Your Site
⭐ Genuinely good signs
  • You publish your CSLB licence number on the site. Most contractors bury it or leave it off. It is a real trust signal and it belongs where it is.
  • You offer customer financing. ⭐ This is the single most interesting thing on your website. Nobody sets up financing for gutter cleaning. It says you are selling work big enough that a homeowner has to think about how to pay for it — and that you have already solved the biggest objection on a high-ticket job.
  • You list ADUs. In Los Angeles that is one of the highest-value residential jobs available, and demand for them is not going away.
  • The site loads, it is readable, and there is a phone number and a form on it.
The Problems, Hardest First
🔴 1. There is no tracking on your website at all

We checked for every normal measurement tool. There is no Google Analytics, no Tag Manager, no Google Ads tag, and no Meta pixel. Nothing.

What that means in practice: you do not know how many people visit your site, where they came from, which pages they read, or how many of them called you. If one of your nine services brings in every enquiry and the other eight bring in none, there is currently no way for you to know that.

⚠️ And it means nobody has ever advertised for you. An ads tag is the first thing any agency or freelancer installs. Its absence tells us this ground is completely untouched — which is good news in that the easy wins are all still sitting there, and bad news in that we would be starting with no history to learn from.

🔴 2. You own a domain but take enquiries at a Gmail address

Your site publishes a @gmail.com address, even though you own allkimi.com. A homeowner deciding whether to let you build a $200,000 ADU in their back garden reads that as a smaller, less established operation than you are.

⭐ This is a thirty-minute fix and it costs almost nothing. It is the cheapest credibility you will ever buy.

⚠️ 3. Nine trades on one website is nine half-arguments

Roofing, textured coating, windows, landscaping, gutters, attic insulation, ADUs, kitchens and bathrooms. Every one of those is a different buyer with a different budget and a different reason to call.

A homeowner researching an ADU wants to see ADUs you have built, what they cost, how long they take, and how you handle permits. On a site that treats ADUs as one of nine equal options, they cannot tell whether you build one a year or one a week.

The fix is not to drop the other eight. It is to decide which one you actually want more of, and give that one the depth the others do not need.

⚠️ 4. The site is thin, and thin sites do not get found

The whole homepage is roughly fifteen hundred words covering nine trades. There is not enough on any single service for Google to understand what you are best at, and not enough for a homeowner to feel confident before they phone you.

What We Could Not Verify, And Are Not Going To Claim
Said plainly, because guessing is worse than asking
  • Your licence status. The number is published on your site. We could not retrieve a readable record from the state board to confirm whether it is currently active, what classifications it carries, or whether you hold workers' compensation or an exemption. Not a concern — simply something we could not read, and something you can tell us in one sentence.
  • “Over ten years of experience.” That is what your site says. We have not independently confirmed it and we are not treating it as a finding either way.
  • Whether you have employees, subcontractors, or neither. This changes what “two jobs a month” would actually require of you, so it matters more than it sounds.
  • How you get work today. Referrals, repeat clients, a directory, word of mouth? Nothing on the site tells us, and it is the thing we most need to know.
Why This Is Not Simply “Buy More Leads”
The honest version

If you are doing one job a month, the bottleneck might not be enquiries at all. There are three genuinely different possibilities and they need three different answers:

  • A. Almost nobody is contacting you. Then the work is getting in front of people — and with zero tracking in place, that starts with being able to measure it.
  • B. People contact you and do not buy. Then more enquiries make your week worse, not better. The work is in what happens after the phone rings.
  • C. You are turning work down, or cannot physically run two jobs at once. Then this is a capacity conversation and marketing would actively hurt you.

⛔ We cannot tell which of the three it is from the outside, and we are not going to pretend otherwise. That is what the call is for. ⭐ But note that B and C are both cheaper to fix than A — so there is a real chance the answer costs you less than you are expecting.

What We Would Want To Know On The Call
  • 1. What is a typical job worth? The single most important number, and the first one we will ask for.
  • 2. Which of the nine services do you actually want more of? And which would you happily never do again?
  • 3. Where does the work come from now? Referrals, repeats, directories, walk-ins?
  • 4. Roughly how many people enquire in a month, and how many become jobs? Even a rough figure changes the plan completely.
  • 5. Could you handle two at once? Who does the work — you, employees, or subcontractors?
  • 6. Have you ever run ads? Your site says no. If you have, we would want to know what happened.
Six questions, and the first one does most of the work. We would rather spend your time on those than on telling you things about your own business that you already know.

You want one more job a month. That is a findable number.

But right now there is nothing in place that could tell you whether you found it.
